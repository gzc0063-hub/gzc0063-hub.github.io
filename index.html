About

From a family farm to field trials and evidence synthesis

I am a PhD student in Weed Science at Auburn University, with a minor in Applied Statistics and a graduate certificate in Data Science. My research centers on herbicide efficacy and weed management in high-residue conservation tillage systems, across row crops and forages.

My work combines 15+ replicated field trials with application technology and data science. I have led data analysis for 21 projects, including four PRISMA-guided meta-analyses, and I build decision-support tools such as the Alabama ESA Mitigation Points Calculator. I work with collaborators at Auburn, USDA-ARS, Penn State, Nebraska and Illinois on joint trials, meta-analyses and grant proposals.

I care about getting research to the people who use it. I have presented weed management recommendations to 200+ growers and advisers through one-to-one communication and extension field days. I am a licensed pesticide applicator in Alabama and an FAA Part 107 pilot, and I received the 2026 CSSA Gerald O. Mott Meritorious Graduate Student Award in Crop Science.

I finish my PhD in Summer 2027 and am available immediately after.

Meta-analyses (PRISMA-guided)

Forage herbicide meta-analysis

+17.9% forage yield

where weeds were controlled · 13 studies, 185 contrasts

Impact of chemical weed control on forage productivity in Southern U.S. permanent pastures. I am leading extraction, analysis and the first draft.

Critical period for weed control (CPWC) in row crops

+59.9% delay

in the critical period for weed control · 103 contrasts, 34 studies, 51 site-years

Influence of agronomic interventions on the critical period for weed control in row crops. In preparation for Weed Technology.

Herbicides and the soil microbiome

Herbicide effects on microbial biomass, respiration and dehydrogenase. I led the whole analysis for this meta-analysis.

Spray drift in UAV applications

Global meta-analysis of spray-drift drivers in UAV applications. I ran the sensitivity analysis and am a co-author.

Field research and work in progress

  • Peanut CPWC field trials. Quantified how five winter cover crop systems shift the critical period for weed control in strip-tilled peanut across three site-years (split-plot design). Cereal rye narrowed the CPWC to 3.2–5.8 weeks.
  • Cotton CPWC field trials. Influence of winter cover crops on the critical period for weed control in conservation tillage cotton systems.
  • Nozzle and spraying-technique trials. Compared spraying techniques for pre-emergence (PRE) herbicide application in high-residue systems, including conventional ground equipment.
  • Drone application (assisting). Assisted with a two-season (2024–2025) on-farm PRE trial comparing drone and ground sprayer application for annual ryegrass control.
  • Amaranthus chemical-management meta-analysis (in progress). PRISMA-guided meta-analysis of chemical management of Amaranthus spp. in U.S. row crops, in collaboration with researchers at Penn State University and the University of Nebraska-Lincoln. Data are extracted and analysis is in progress.
